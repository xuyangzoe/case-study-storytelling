Story contract故事契约

Central question核心问题
Why did a feature stall for four months and then ship across three platforms in two weeks — with the same designer and the same brief? 为什么同一个设计师、同一份需求,一个功能先卡了四个月,之后却在两周内完成了三端交付?
Recommended argument推荐的故事主张
Because the blocker was never the design — it was that no two people in the room meant the same thing by "cheer." Four months of rejected versions came from a model nobody had agreed on; once a workshop finally produced one — spend more, get more premium, get more visible — the entire system resolved in two weeks. The two-week delivery is the proof that the missing thing was a shared definition, not craft. 因为卡住项目的从来不是设计,而是屋子里没有任何两个人对"cheer"的理解是一致的。四个月被否的版本,源自一个从未达成共识的模型;而当工作坊终于产出这个模型——花得越多,越高级,越被看见——整套系统在两周内就收敛了。这两周的交付,正是"缺的是共同定义、而不是设计能力"的证明。

Findings核心发现

  • The two-week delivery is your strongest evidence and it is currently buried. Same designer, same brief, three platforms — the only variable that changed was a shared model. Nothing else in this case proves as much, and it needs no internal data to stand up. 两周交付是你最强的证据,而它目前被埋没了。同一个设计师、同一份需求、三个平台——唯一变化的变量是有了共识模型。这个 case 里没有任何别的东西能证明这么多,而且它不需要任何内部数据就能成立。
  • Your week-five draft already carried the shipped logic. That is the most important fact in this case — and the most dangerous to tell. As "I was right and got blocked," it sinks the whole thing. As "I had the logic but couldn't yet make the room hold it," it becomes the sharpest version of your argument: it explains why you couldn't answer the head's questions, and it makes the closing lesson true rather than tidy. 你第五周的草稿里已经有了上线版本的逻辑。这是整个 case 中最重要的一个事实——也是最危险的一个。讲成"我早就是对的、只是被压住了",整个 case 就毁了;讲成"我有那个逻辑,但当时还没能力让整个房间接住它",它就成了你论点最锋利的版本:它解释了你为什么答不出 head 的问题,也让结尾的教训变得真实,而不只是漂亮。
  • Credit the workshop accurately and you lose nothing. Your mentor led the synthesis and had been leaning this way in your early discussions — say so. The half that is genuinely yours is larger anyway: the direction was already in your rejected drafts, and you were the one who could build it out once it had language. That claim survives any follow-up; "I ran the workshop" does not. 如实交代工作坊的归属,你不会损失任何东西。总结是 mentor 带的,而且她在你们早期讨论时就一直倾向这个方向——照实说。真正属于你的那一半反而更大:这个方向本就存在于你被否的草稿里,而当它终于有了语言之后,你是那个能把它落地的人。这个说法经得起任何追问;"工作坊是我带的"经不起。

Kicks — KICK's Cheer & Gifting SystemKicks——KICK 平台的打赏与送礼系统

A virtual currency viewers buy and send to support streamers live — KICK's first monetization system built on a moment. 一种观众可购买、用于实时支持主播的虚拟货币——KICK 第一个建立在"当下时刻"之上的变现系统。

Associate Product Designer · sole designer, web + mobile + TV · Mar–Aug · shipped and live Associate Product Designer · 唯一设计师,Web + 移动端 + TV · 3 月至 8 月 · 已上线
SLIDE-01stated-only仅口述
Every Kicks feature KICK builds from here would inherit whatever model we set now.从这一刻起,KICK 之后所有基于 Kicks 的功能,都会继承我们此刻定下的模型。
Content内容
Viewers reacted to live moments in real time but had no lightweight way to make that support visible; KICK had no revenue mechanic tied to it. Kicks was the platform's first virtual currency, which meant it wasn't only a feature — it was the model every later feature would price, tier, and reward against. Getting it wrong was expensive in a way a single screen never is. 观众会对直播中的精彩时刻实时产生反应,却没有轻量的方式让这份支持被看见;而 KICK 也没有与之绑定的营收机制。Kicks 是平台的第一套虚拟货币,这意味着它不只是一个功能——它是之后每一个功能在计价、分档、设置奖励时都要参照的模型。做错的代价,远不是"一个界面做砸了"可以相比的。
Why this slide为何设为此页
Raises the stake honestly. It also pre-explains why four senior people kept rejecting it — the cost of a wrong model justified the scrutiny.诚实地抬高了利害关系。它同时提前解释了为什么四位资深角色反复否决——模型错了代价太大,严苛是合理的。

The PRD specified Twitch's Cheering with Bits, so V1 reproduced the mechanic and logic exactly, with the UI reworked — tiers three per row, and a tap animation popping "+1" as you added a Kick. Mentor, design lead, design head, and the chief creative officer all reviewed the low-fi prototype and said to keep going. The notes were font sizes and spacing. PRD 明确要求对齐 Twitch 的 Cheering with Bits,所以 V1 完整复刻了机制与逻辑,只重做了 UI——档位每行三个,点击加一个 Kick 时弹出"+1"动效。Mentor、design lead、design head 和首席创意官都看过这版低保真原型,一致表示可以继续。反馈集中在字号和间距上。

Content内容
Cheer, Kicks, cheer message, cheer amount, cheermote — five terms, and every reviewer held a slightly different version of each. I couldn't define them cleanly under questioning. Neither could the room: when the mechanic was whiteboarded to align us, the PM had to correct several of the definitions on the board. That was the moment the problem changed shape for me. I had been iterating a design on top of a model nobody actually shared. Cheer、Kicks、cheer message、cheer amount、cheermote——五个术语,而每位评审者对每一个的理解都略有不同。在追问下我无法清晰定义它们。但整个屋子也做不到:当有人把机制画上白板试图对齐时,PM 不得不当场纠正白板上的多处定义。就是那一刻,问题在我眼里变了形状——我一直在一个根本没有共识的模型之上迭代设计。
Why this slide为何设为此页

Content内容
One of those five rejected mobile drafts already carried the model that shipped in August. Different UI, same underlying logic. It was rejected, and I let it go — because I could draw the idea but I couldn't yet explain why it answered the objections. That is the honest gap. An idea only I can defend isn't a proposal yet; it's a drawing. Two other calls from the same weeks: I took a proposed direction to two engineers who said it couldn't be built as drawn and found no shipping product doing it that way, and I argued against splitting mobile from web on KICK's own ~95% parity standard. 那五版被否的移动端草稿里,有一版已经带着八月上线的那个模型——UI 不同,底层逻辑一样。它被否了,我也就放下了,因为我画得出这个想法,却还说不清它为什么回答了那些质疑。这就是当时真实的差距:一个只有我自己能辩护的想法,还算不上一个提案,它只是一张图。同期还有另外两个判断:我把某个方向拿去问了两位开发,他们说按图做不了,我也没找到任何已上线产品这么做过;以及我依据 KICK 自己约 95% 的一致性标准,反对移动端脱离 Web 端。

The first reads as judgment; the second ends the conversation.这一页的所有内容都必须落成"诊断",而不是"我早说过"。说你画了什么、核实了什么、提出了什么——以及你当时还论证不了它。绝不要说谁错了。前者听起来是判断力,后者会直接终结这场谈话。

SLIDE-07supported有据可查
The workshop didn't find a new idea — it gave the one already in the room a shared sentence.工作坊并没有找到新想法——它只是给房间里早已存在的那个想法,一句共同的话。
Content内容
In July the team ran a workshop — three PMs, my mentor, and me — and my mentor led the synthesis. She had been leaning this way in our early discussions; the direction sat in my rejected week-five draft. What the session produced wasn't a discovery, it was a model everyone could hold at once: tiers as the axis, more spend earning a more premium treatment, and premium meaning more visible — across chat message, pin, overlay, and badge. One sentence answered the head's second and third objections. That's why it held where eleven versions hadn't. 七月,团队组织了一场工作坊——三位 PM、我的 mentor 和我——由我的 mentor 带领做总结。她在我们早期讨论时就一直倾向这个方向;而这个方向本身,就躺在我第五周那版被否的草稿里。这场会议产出的不是一个发现,而是一个所有人都能同时握住的模型:以档位为轴,花得越多,呈现越高级,而"高级"意味着越被看见——覆盖聊天消息、置顶、浮层和徽章。一句话,同时回答了 head 的第二和第三个质疑。这就是它能站住、而此前十一个版本站不住的原因。

Week one, Kicks did seven-figure transaction volume — and repeat sends clustered almost entirely on the cheapest tier.
