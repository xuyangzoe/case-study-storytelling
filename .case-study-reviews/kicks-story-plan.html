Story contract故事契约

Central question核心问题
Why did a feature stall for four months and then ship across three platforms in two weeks — with the same designer and the same brief? 为什么同一个设计师、同一份需求,一个功能先卡了四个月,之后却在两周内完成了三端交付?
Recommended argument推荐的故事主张
Because the blocker was never the design — it was that no two people in the room meant the same thing by "cheer." Four months of rejected versions came from a model nobody had agreed on; once a workshop finally produced one — spend more, get more premium, get more visible — the entire system resolved in two weeks. The two-week delivery is the proof that the missing thing was a shared definition, not craft. 因为卡住项目的从来不是设计,而是屋子里没有任何两个人对"cheer"的理解是一致的。四个月被否的版本,源自一个从未达成共识的模型;而当工作坊终于产出这个模型——花得越多,越高级,越被看见——整套系统在两周内就收敛了。这两周的交付,正是"缺的是共同定义、而不是设计能力"的证明。

Findings核心发现

  • The two-week delivery is your strongest evidence and it is currently buried. Same designer, same brief, three platforms — the only variable that changed was a shared model. Nothing else in this case proves as much, and it needs no internal data to stand up. 两周交付是你最强的证据,而它目前被埋没了。同一个设计师、同一份需求、三个平台——唯一变化的变量是有了共识模型。这个 case 里没有任何别的东西能证明这么多,而且它不需要任何内部数据就能成立。
  • Keep "I had the idea early" out of the told story. It can only be taken on trust, and it invites the one question you don't want — "then why didn't it go anywhere?" What you can prove is the opposite skill: you took a reframe in a room, changed how you were thinking, and shipped three platforms in two weeks. Designers with ideas are common; designers who absorb a hard reframe after six rejections and then converge fast are not. If an interviewer asks whether you'd explored that direction earlier, answer honestly — responsive is accurate, volunteered is credit-claiming. 不要把"我早就有这个想法"放进要讲的故事里。它只能靠听众相信你,而且会招来你最不想听到的追问——"那为什么没推动成?"你能证明的是相反的能力:你在会议中接住了一次重构、改变了自己的思路,并在两周内交付了三端。有想法的设计师很多;被否六次之后还能吸收一次艰难的重构、并迅速收敛的设计师不多。如果面试官主动问你早期是否探索过那个方向,如实回答——被问到时说是准确,主动提起就成了争功。
  • Credit the workshop accurately and you lose nothing. Your mentor led the synthesis and had been leaning this way in your early discussions — say so. The half that is genuinely yours is larger anyway: the direction was already in your rejected drafts, and you were the one who could build it out once it had language. That claim survives any follow-up; "I ran the workshop" does not. 如实交代工作坊的归属,你不会损失任何东西。总结是 mentor 带的,而且她在你们早期讨论时就一直倾向这个方向——照实说。真正属于你的那一半反而更大:这个方向本就存在于你被否的草稿里,而当它终于有了语言之后,你是那个能把它落地的人。这个说法经得起任何追问;"工作坊是我带的"经不起。

Associate Product Designer · sole designer, web + mobile + TV · Mar–Aug · shipped and live Associate Product Designer · 唯一设计师,Web + 移动端 + TV · 3 月至 8 月 · 已上线
SLIDE-01stated-only仅口述
Every Kicks feature KICK builds from here would inherit whatever model we set now.从这一刻起,KICK 之后所有基于 Kicks 的功能,都会继承我们此刻定下的模型。
Content内容
Kicks was KICK's first virtual currency, so it wasn't only a feature — it was the model every later feature would price, tier, and reward against. The behavior it had to shift was specific on both sides. Viewer: from reacting privately to a moment, to being seen backing it in front of the room. Streamer: from an audience that could only watch, to one whose enthusiasm converts into income attached to a particular moment. Getting that wrong is expensive in a way a single screen never is. Kicks 是 KICK 的第一套虚拟货币,所以它不只是一个功能——它是之后每一个功能在计价、分档、设置奖励时都要参照的模型。它要撬动的行为,在两端都很具体。观众端:从"私下为某个瞬间叫好",变成"在全场面前被看见自己在支持它"。主播端:从只能观看的观众,变成一群热情可以转化为收入、并且这笔收入明确挂在某个具体瞬间上的观众。这件事做错的代价,远不是"一个界面做砸了"可以相比的。
Why this slide为何设为此页
The behavior sentence is the yardstick every later decision is measured against — it has to be specific enough to be wrong. It also pre-explains why four senior people kept rejecting: a wrong model here propagates.这句"行为陈述"是后面每一个决策的标尺——它必须具体到"有可能是错的"才算数。它同时提前解释了为什么四位资深角色反复否决:这里模型错了,是会往下蔓延的。

SLIDE-06stated-only仅口述
In the workshop, tiers stopped being price points and became an axis of visibility.在工作坊上,档位不再是价格点,而变成了一条"可见度"的轴。
Content内容
In July the team ran a workshop — three PMs, my mentor, and me — and my mentor led the synthesis. I went in holding the model I'd inherited from the PRD: Kicks are a currency, tiers are price points, and the design problem is the spending flow. I came out holding a different one: tiers are an axis of visibility. Spend more, get a more premium treatment; premium means occupying more of the room — chat message, pin, overlay, badge. That reframe answered the head's second and third objections in one sentence, which is why it held where eleven versions hadn't. 七月,团队组织了一场工作坊——三位 PM、我的 mentor 和我——由我的 mentor 带领做总结。我带着从 PRD 继承来的模型走进去:Kicks 是一种货币,档位是价格点,设计问题在于消费流程。我带着另一个模型走出来:档位是一条可见度的轴。花得越多,呈现越高级;而"高级"意味着占据更多的场面——聊天消息、置顶、浮层、徽章。这个重构用一句话同时回答了 head 的第二和第三个质疑,这正是它能站住、而此前十一个版本站不住的原因。

SLIDE-11supported有据可查
What we kept from Twitch, what we replaced, and where we went that it doesn't.哪些沿用了 Twitch、哪些被替换掉了,以及我们走到了它没去过的地方。
Kept — deliberately刻意沿用的
The currency-and-tier mechanic, the chat-command entry, the panel model. Viewers move between platforms; making them relearn how to support someone buys nothing. Keeping the mechanic familiar is what freed the invention to go somewhere that mattered.货币与档位机制、聊天指令入口、面板模型。观众会在不同平台之间流动,让他们重新学习"怎么支持一个人"换不来任何东西。正是保留了熟悉的机制,才把创新的余地腾给了真正重要的地方。
Replaced替换掉的
Twitch dead-ends an empty balance — "you have 0 Bits and cannot send a Cheer." Ours rounds up to the nearest bundle inside the flow, with an adjustable quantity and a per-transaction cap, so the impulse survives the top-up. Two, both at the moment money is on the line. Three things, each one this project taught me the hard way.
